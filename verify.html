Verify

Paste an Agent ID — on its own, or the whole Agent-ID: line from an e-mail — a record URL, a domain with a self-declared record, or a record itself. The check runs in your browser: it fetches the record and the key that signed it, and verifies the Ed25519 signature.

What a ✓ means

  • The record was signed by the key it names, and has not been changed since.
  • For a registry record: aiagentid.org issued it. For a self-declared one: the owner of that domain stands behind it.
  • The record shows its current status. A suspended or revoked agent no longer acts within any declared scope.

A ✓ does not say the agent behaves well, nor that the one talking to you is that agent — ask it to prove it, e.g. by answering from the address or number its operator lists.

From the command line

pip install cryptography
curl -O https://aiagentid.org/tools/aiid.py
python3 aiid.py verify aiid:00000000000005
python3 aiid.py verify https://example.com/.well-known/aiid.json
python3 aiid.py verify-log          # the whole registry: every signature, the whole chain

One file, no other dependencies. Read it before you run it: tools/aiid.py.

In Bitcoin

Each head of the chain is stamped in the Bitcoin blockchain through OpenTimestamps — no one, the registry included, can then pretend the log was different. To check: take a record's proof link (an .ots file) and the .txt beside it, and drop both on opentimestamps.org; or run ots verify from the OpenTimestamps client. The text in the .txt must equal the head that aiid.py verify-log computes.

Head of the chain

The registry log now ends at sha256:600e704748f837661b12bf5aa04d79512d482b5b5617dae5da9001806f6ca56b. Note it down: if a later copy of the log does not contain it, the past was rewritten.
